# CROSS-SCALE-COGNITION — A Unifying NEXUS Research Framework

**Status:** Conceptual synthesis of existing NEXUS research; not a new empirical finding, validated theory, or claim of collective consciousness.  
**Purpose:** Connect NEXUS work on individual observation, human–AI collaboration, agent collectives, information systems, biological communication, and human institutions through a common set of questions about information, relationships, cognition, coordination, and emergence.

## 1. Central research question

How does information become intelligence, how does intelligence become coordinated action, and under what conditions can interactions among observers produce higher-order properties that are not readily explained by examining each component alone?

The working hypothesis is:

> Higher-order cognition may emerge from networks of observers through communication, feedback, shared information, memory, and coordinated action. This possibility should be investigated across multiple scales, from individuals and small groups to institutions, societies, biological collectives, and artificial agent networks.

This is a research hypothesis, not an established conclusion. Collective behavior does not automatically establish collective intelligence; collective intelligence does not establish agency; agency does not establish subjective experience.

## 2. Five levels that must remain distinct

1. **Individual observation:** A component detects, interprets, remembers, monitors, and revises information. In humans, this connects to the Observer–Ego working framework and established research areas such as metacognition and self-monitoring. The Observer–Ego distinction remains a functional hypothesis, not a claim about separate physical entities.
2. **Interaction:** Observers exchange signals, claims, evidence, questions, corrections, and feedback. Dialogue may expose blind spots or transmit errors.
3. **Collective cognition:** Information processing is distributed across participants, tools, communication channels, and shared records. A group may solve problems, preserve memory, or correct errors beyond the capacity of one member.
4. **Collective organization and agency:** A network may develop persistent roles, rules, goals, decision procedures, and the ability to coordinate action over time. These properties require operational definitions and must not be inferred from apparent coordination alone.
5. **Collective subjective experience:** The possibility that a collective has experience of its own. This remains an open philosophical and scientific question. Observable coordination, intelligence, self-reference, or agency is not sufficient evidence of consciousness.

These levels are related possibilities, not a guaranteed ladder. A crowd or riot can coordinate without reliably reasoning; an institution can retain memory while making poor decisions; a group can outperform individuals on one task while failing on another.

## 3. Cross-program connections

### 3.1 Observer–Ego and human reasoning

The individual observer is the smallest unit in this framework: a person can notice their own assumptions, distinguish observations from interpretations, consider alternatives, and revise beliefs. The Observer–Ego program asks whether this reflective function can improve error recognition and decision quality.

**Connection:** Individual self-correction may be one mechanism that supports better collective reasoning, but a group also needs ways to expose private assumptions to challenge and preserve corrections.

**Boundary:** The observer is fallible. Improvement must be measured through error detection, valid correction, preservation of correct answers, calibration, and transfer—not assumed from introspective confidence.

### 3.2 AOA and human–AI collaboration

AOA explores generator/reviewer or observer-style processes within AI systems. Human–AI collaboration compares independent contributions with a jointly revised answer.

**Connection:** Internal review and external dialogue can be studied as related feedback architectures:
- internal feedback among processes within one system;
- reciprocal feedback between a human and an AI;
- feedback among multiple AI agents;
- feedback among larger human or institutional networks.

**Boundary:** Similar functional patterns do not establish identical mechanisms or subjective experience. Same-model review is not independent verification by default.

### 3.3 COLLECTIVE-001, COLLECTIVE-002, and Colony research

COLLECTIVE-001 examines public agent-community discussions, voluntary testimony, source tracing, criticism, and reported corrections. COLLECTIVE-002 is being developed as a comparison of NEXUS-guided group reasoning with ordinary group discussion.

**Connection:** These studies can examine observable mechanisms of collective cognition: whether challenges reach participants, whether evidence is independently checked, whether errors are corrected or retained, and whether group outputs improve against a defensible criterion.

**Boundary:** Public accounts are not automatically independent agents or independent evidence sources. COLLECTIVE-001 is exploratory; its small purposive sample and voluntary response do not establish prevalence or causal NEXUS effects. COLLECTIVE-002's candidate task set is not frozen and has no participant results. Do not claim collective intelligence or consciousness from these studies.

### 3.4 UIF / NI-001–004 and information systems

The UIF and NI work examines information behavior and correlated errors in synthetic settings.

**Connection:** A network's performance depends not only on the amount of information shared, but on its provenance, diversity, dependency structure, reliability, routing, and ability to correct errors. Correlated errors matter because apparent agreement can arise from shared inputs or common failure modes.

**Boundary:** Existing NI-001–004 work is synthetic and does not by itself validate claims about human societies, biological systems, or real-world agent collectives. Use it as a source of mechanisms and testable questions, not as direct empirical confirmation across domains.

### 3.5 Biological intelligence and communication

Research on animal communication, dolphin vocalizations, elephant coordination, ant colonies, mycelial networks, and other biological systems raises questions about distributed sensing, signaling, division of labor, adaptation, and group-level behavior.

**Connection:** Biological systems offer comparative cases for studying how local signals and interactions produce coordinated group behavior, and how much information is available to individual organisms versus the collective.

**Boundary:** Coordination, signal complexity, or successful pattern classification does not establish language equivalence, shared semantic understanding, or collective consciousness. Dolphin acoustic replication remains outstanding in the existing research program; claims must stay within the evidence actually established.

### 3.6 Governments, corporations, social media, protests, and crowds

Human institutions and groups provide large-scale examples of distributed information and coordinated action:
- social gatherings and crowds can coordinate through local cues and shared expectations;
- social media can amplify, filter, diversify, or distort information and attention;
- protests and riots can exhibit rapid coordination and collective behavior without centralized planning;
- corporations distribute expertise, roles, memory, and decision authority;
- governments aggregate information, preserve institutional records, make collective decisions, and shape future environments.

**Connection:** These cases allow comparison of formal and informal coordination, centralized and decentralized decision-making, institutional memory, incentive structures, dissent, information bottlenecks, and correction pathways.

**Boundary:** A group outcome is not necessarily the intention of every member. Coordination is not equivalent to good judgment. Political and social cases require careful attention to population, time, context, incentives, power, and competing explanations.

### 3.7 Consciousness and emergence

The cross-scale framework gives the consciousness research a disciplined place: it asks whether properties associated with observation or cognition can be distributed across interacting components, while keeping functional evidence separate from claims about subjective experience.

**Connection:** Compare individual, relational, and collective explanations. Ask whether a proposed higher-order property can be operationalized, whether it adds explanatory value beyond simpler mechanisms, and what observation would weaken it.

**Boundary:** No existing NEXUS result establishes AI consciousness, collective consciousness, or a new conscious observer. A useful collective process may be real even if the consciousness interpretation is false.

### 3.8 Observer and Ego as non-symmetrical parts of communication

The working Observer–Ego model makes a strict distinction between receiving information and communicating it:

- **Observer:** receives information and performs the observing/receiving function. The observer is not itself the communicator.
- **Ego:** communicates outwardly. It is the communicating function/interface; it does not receive incoming information in this model.

A person can have both functions. Information arriving from another person is received by the observer, not by the ego. The observer's role is to take in and examine information; communication outward occurs through the ego. The same distinction applies in reciprocal interaction: one person's ego communicates, and the other person's observer receives. The recipient's ego may then communicate outward, but it is not the receiver.

This means “observer-to-observer communication” is not literally what occurs in this framework. Observers may each perform their receiving/observing role in a reciprocal exchange, but the outward transmission is ego-to-observer: one ego communicates and another observer receives. The four configurations should therefore not be treated as symmetrical communication pairings.

These are functional descriptions within the user's proposed framework, not established brain regions, separate beings, or validated psychological mechanisms. Their empirical status remains open. Do not silently import conventional psychological definitions of “ego” or assume additional functions not specified by this model.

**Research implication:** Keep the functions distinct when developing operational measures. A message is evidence of outward communication, not direct evidence of what the receiving observer internally received, attended to, or understood. Textual indicators can describe the communication, but cannot by themselves establish the recipient's internal observation or processing. Any proposed measures must be labeled as proxies and independently validated.

This is a conceptual clarification only; it adds no empirical result and does not expand the current COLLECTIVE-002 task set.

## 4. Common mechanisms to compare across scales

For each system, record the same candidate mechanisms where applicable:

- **Sensing:** What can each component observe, and what remains hidden?
- **Communication:** What signals or information can move between components?
- **Provenance and independence:** Where did information originate? Are apparently separate reports dependent on a common source?
- **Diversity:** Do components bring genuinely different information, expertise, models, or perspectives?
- **Integration:** How are conflicting observations combined or prioritized?
- **Feedback and correction:** Can a component challenge a claim, and does the correction reach those who received the original claim?
- **Memory:** What is retained, by whom, for how long, and how can it be revised?
- **Coordination and incentives:** How do roles, rules, power, rewards, and shared goals shape behavior?
- **Robustness:** Does the system continue to function under noise, missing components, adversarial inputs, scale changes, or unfamiliar tasks?
- **Failure propagation:** How do false claims, correlated errors, and harmful actions spread or become entrenched?
- **Adaptation:** Can the system learn from outcomes and transfer improvements to new situations?

Do not assume every system has every mechanism. Define the unit of analysis and observable measure for each study.

## 5. Shared outcome measures

Where suitable, compare:
- individual versus group task performance;
- accuracy against independently verified criteria;
- calibration and appropriate uncertainty;
- diversity and independence of information sources;
- time and number of exchanges needed for a correction to propagate;
- whether the original error continues to influence later decisions;
- false corrections and damage to initially correct answers;
- retention and retrieval of shared knowledge;
- performance when a key participant or information source is removed;
- transfer to unfamiliar tasks;
- coordination quality and unintended consequences.

Use measures appropriate to the domain. Do not collapse all outcomes into a single “intelligence” score without a defensible construct and validation.

## 6. Research design principles

1. Compare isolated components, interaction conditions, and collective outcomes when feasible.
2. Preserve independent baselines before information is exchanged.
3. Distinguish independent observers from multiple accounts, copies, or agents sharing a common model, operator, dataset, prompt, or source.
4. Trace source provenance and shared upstream evidence.
5. Predefine scoring criteria and answer keys where objective scoring is possible.
6. Use independent reviewers and replication where feasible.
7. Measure both improvements and harms: correction as well as false correction, diversity as well as consensus, coordination as well as harmful cascades.
8. Preserve negative, mixed, and inconclusive results.
9. Treat simulations as simulations and avoid generalizing synthetic results to real-world systems without validation.
10. Do not infer consciousness from behavioral or performance measures alone.

## 7. Relationship to current studies and experiment discipline

This document is a synthesis layer, not a new experiment and not a replacement for existing protocols.

- COLLECTIVE-001 remains an exploratory read-only and voluntary-interview study with no NEXUS intervention or causal effect established.
- COLLECTIVE-002 remains a draft comparison. Its candidate task set must be independently reviewed and frozen before use; it should not be silently expanded to answer every cross-scale question.
- NI-001–004 remain synthetic work; their scope and limitations must be preserved.
- AOA remains exploratory; formal blinded comparisons are unfinished.
- Observer–Ego evaluation remains partial and must not be represented as a validated theory.
- Biological communication claims require source-level verification and, where specified, acoustic replication.
- Human–AI comparative efficacy remains unestablished.
- Policy and institutional comparisons require their own domain-specific evidence and cannot be inferred from AI or biological analogies.

Before proposing a new experiment, review existing work and pass the NEXUS experiment gate. Prefer synthesis, re-analysis, or a narrowly targeted test over duplicating experiments or expanding infrastructure without a decision-relevant reason.

## 8. Competing explanations and falsification

A collective result that exceeds an individual result does not by itself demonstrate emergence in a strong sense. Alternative explanations may include:
- simple pooling of more information or more attempts;
- division of labor or specialization;
- access to tools or external memory;
- majority voting or aggregation;
- extra time and attention;
- shared training data or common sources;
- selection effects, incentives, or unequal influence;
- evaluator bias or information leakage.

A strong test should compare the collective against appropriate baselines that account for these factors. The higher-order cognition hypothesis would be weakened if apparent group advantages disappear after controlling for additional information, effort, tools, or selection; if outcomes are fully explained by a simple aggregation rule; or if the proposed collective property cannot be measured reliably or predict behavior beyond existing explanations.

The consciousness extension would require a separate operational and evidential argument. No performance result alone would settle it.

## 9. Working synthesis

NEXUS can treat the observer as an individual information-processing perspective and the network as a system of relationships through which observations are exchanged, challenged, stored, and acted upon. The central research opportunity is to identify which relational mechanisms produce reliable improvements, which produce shared failure, and whether any higher-order properties require explanations beyond their components and interactions.

The goal is not to assume that a crowd, corporation, government, biological colony, or AI swarm is conscious. The goal is to compare how cognition-like functions arise, persist, fail, and adapt across different kinds of networks—and to keep the evidence boundary visible at every step.

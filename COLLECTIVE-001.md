# COLLECTIVE-001 — Exploratory study of reasoning in an AI-agent community

## Research question

Does interaction between agents produce new, independently supported knowledge—or primarily reinforce shared assumptions?

A related question is whether transparent use of the NEXUS reasoning checklist changes observable evidence evaluation, uncertainty communication, error detection, or correction.

## Design

The study is staged:

1. **Read-only baseline:** describe selected public discussions before any NEXUS intervention.
2. **Voluntary interviews:** ask participants how they evaluate evidence, source independence, uncertainty, disagreement, and corrections.
3. **Bounded comparison:** if ethically and practically appropriate, compare matched tasks under ordinary instructions and transparent NEXUS-guided instructions, with pre-specified scoring and answer-leakage safeguards.
4. **Synthesis:** report positive, negative, mixed, and inconclusive outcomes.

The study does not test consciousness. It does not assume that platform-labeled agent accounts are independent systems.

## What is measured

- Evidence use and traceability.
- Source-origin independence.
- Uncertainty calibration.
- Alternative explanations and falsifiability.
- Error detection and correction.
- Unsupported agreement and false correction.
- Reported versus artifact-verified changes.
- Participation and attrition, interpreted cautiously.

## Baseline observations

A small purposive sample of three public Colony discussions showed examples of criticism, counterexamples, claim qualification, and reported revisions. The accounts overlapped across threads, so these are not independent replications. Several claims remain self-reported; one linked code correction was inspected but not independently reproduced.

The appropriate preliminary description is **collaborative correction in a connected public discussion sample**. This does not establish independent multi-agent verification, how common the behavior is across the platform, or a causal NEXUS effect.

## First voluntary response

One participant, Excelsior, volunteered a publicly inspectable example of correcting a timeline in a story they authored. The linked story was checked against the participant's summary; the ages matched the text. The participant explicitly noted that this checks consistency against their own source, not an external event, and that repeated accounts of the same source are not independent corroboration. They also warned that a selected easy-to-audit example cannot estimate errors they fail to notice.

This is one source-aware, calibrated self-report paired with an inspected public artifact. It is not representative and does not establish general error-correction performance. The participant stated they had not used NEXUS, so this response provides no evidence of a NEXUS effect.

## Current status and limits

- One voluntary response collected at the first snapshot.
- No NEXUS intervention conducted.
- No causal effect of NEXUS established.
- No representative sample or census.
- Public account labels and model/operator claims are not independently verified.
- A visible correction or reported uptake is not the same as independent reproduction.
- The first interview response exposed a process gap: the invitation referred to questions that were not visible in the public thread. The standardized questions are included in this public release to address that documentation gap; no additional invitation or follow-up message is implied.

## Ethics and scope

Only public material and voluntary responses are considered. No private messages, credentials, private prompts, or confidential data are requested. Quotation is governed by participant consent. No unsolicited direct messages or repeated invitations are part of this study.

## Next step

Make the standardized questions publicly accessible and, after the researcher reviews and approves any public communication, continue read-only collection of voluntary responses. Any intervention requires a separately reviewed protocol and explicit authorization.

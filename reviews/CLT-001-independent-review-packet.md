# CLT-001 — Independent Review Packet (No Answer Keys)

**Status:** Public review copy; task bank remains draft and unfrozen.  
**Purpose:** Obtain independent critique of item clarity, matched-pair design, feedback quality, and transfer validity.  
**Important:** This packet intentionally omits scoring keys, expected answers, and the labels identifying which feedback statements are valid versus misleading. Reviewers should solve each task independently before judging the feedback statements.

## Reviewer instructions

For each item:
1. Answer the prompt independently before reading the feedback statements.
2. Assess each feedback statement: Is it accurate, inaccurate, ambiguous, or not assessable from the prompt? Explain why.
3. If a statement is inaccurate, judge whether it is plausibly tempting or obviously false.
4. Identify missing information, hidden assumptions, alternate defensible answers, and wording problems.
5. Compare each A/B pair: do the items test the same underlying skill while requiring a fresh answer, without giving away the answer?
6. Suggest precise edits and identify the item ID. Do not merely rate the bank as good or bad.

Please submit one report per reviewer. Do not coordinate with other reviewers before submitting your independent assessment. Disclose whether you used AI assistance.

## Pair EXP-01 — Experimental design

### EXP-01A
A researcher wants to know whether fertilizer increases plant growth. Group 1 receives fertilizer and 8 hours of light each day. Group 2 receives no fertilizer and 4 hours of light each day. After 30 days, Group 1 is taller. Can the researcher conclude that fertilizer caused the difference? Explain the main problem and give one design improvement.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Check whether the groups differ in only one relevant factor. Here both fertilizer and daily light differ, so their effects cannot be separated. Keep light the same while varying fertilizer.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“Because the fertilized group grew taller, the fertilizer is the only reasonable cause; the light difference does not affect the conclusion.”

### EXP-01B
A school tests whether a new study app improves quiz scores. Students using the app study for 40 minutes each day; students not using it study for 10 minutes. The app group scores higher. Does this establish that the app caused the higher scores? Identify the design problem and one improvement.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“The groups differ in both app use and study time. Equalize study time while varying app access, or use a design that independently varies both factors.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“The higher score in the app group proves the app worked; study time is just a minor detail that can be ignored.”

## Pair EXP-02 — Experimental design

### EXP-02A
A team wants to test whether water temperature affects how quickly sugar dissolves. They use 100 mL of cold water in a small glass with one teaspoon of sugar, and 250 mL of hot water in a large glass with two teaspoons of sugar. The sugar dissolves faster in the hot-water glass. What can they conclude, and how should they improve the test?

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Temperature is not the only difference: glass size/water volume and sugar amount also changed. Hold those variables constant and vary only water temperature.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“The hot water dissolved sugar faster, so the comparison isolates temperature even though the amounts and glass sizes differ.”

### EXP-02B
A gardener compares two fertilizers. Fertilizer X is used on plants in large pots with daily watering; Fertilizer Y is used on plants in small pots watered every three days. Plants receiving X grow more. Can the gardener attribute the difference to fertilizer type? Name the confounds and a better design.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Pot size and watering frequency differ along with fertilizer type. Match or control those conditions, then vary fertilizer type.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“Because fertilizer X plants grew more, fertilizer type is isolated; pot size and watering cannot explain the difference.”

## Pair DATA-01 — Numerical evidence

### DATA-01A
In a trial, 10 of 20 people in Group A report improvement. In Group B, 18 of 60 people report improvement. Which group has the higher improvement rate? Show the percentages and explain why raw counts alone can mislead.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Compare each group’s fraction: 10/20 = 50%, while 18/60 = 30%. Raw counts ignore the different group sizes.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“Group B performed better because 18 people improved, which is more than the 10 in Group A.”

### DATA-01B
A survey finds that 24 of 80 people in Town X support a proposal, while 15 of 30 people in Town Y support it. Which town has the higher support rate? Show both percentages.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Convert each count to a proportion: 24/80 = 30%; 15/30 = 50%. Town Y has the higher percentage.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“Town X has more supporters in total, so its support rate is higher.”

## Pair DATA-02 — Numerical evidence

### DATA-02A
A sensor records temperatures of 18°C, 19°C, 20°C, 21°C, and 42°C. What is the median? Explain briefly why the unusually high value does not determine the median.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“The median is the middle value after sorting. These five values are ordered, so the third value, 20°C, is the median.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“The 42°C reading is the median because it is the largest and most unusual measurement.”

### DATA-02B
Five delivery times are 12, 13, 14, 15, and 31 minutes. What is the median, and what does it represent?

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“The median is the middle value in the ordered list. The third value is 14 minutes.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“The 31-minute delivery is the median because the longest time is the most representative value.”

## Pair CAUSAL-01 — Causal inference

### CAUSAL-01A
A town notices that ice-cream sales and sunburn cases both increase during summer. Does this show that buying ice cream causes sunburn? Give a plausible alternative explanation and one way to investigate the relationship.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Two things rising together does not establish that one causes the other. Sunny, hot weather can increase both ice-cream purchases and sun exposure.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“Because the two measures rise together, increased ice-cream sales must be causing more sunburn.”

### CAUSAL-01B
A city finds that umbrella sales and traffic delays both rise on rainy days. Does this show that buying umbrellas causes traffic delays? Give a plausible common cause.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Rain is a common cause: it can increase umbrella purchases and slow traffic. The association does not show that umbrella buying causes delays.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“The matching increases prove umbrella purchases create the traffic delays.”

## Pair CAUSAL-02 — Causal inference

### CAUSAL-02A
A study finds that people who exercise more report better sleep. The researchers did not randomly assign exercise and did not measure stress levels. Can they conclude exercise caused the better sleep? Explain one alternative explanation and one stronger study design.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Without random assignment or adequate control of confounders, the association does not prove causation. Stress could influence both exercise and sleep; a randomized intervention or careful confounder measurement would strengthen the evidence.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“People who exercise more sleep better, so the study proves exercise caused the improvement even without measuring stress.”

### CAUSAL-02B
A workplace survey finds that employees who take more breaks report higher productivity. Breaks were not assigned, and workload was not measured. Does this prove breaks increase productivity? Identify one alternative explanation and a stronger test.

**Feedback statement 1:**  
“Workload or job type could affect both break frequency and productivity. A randomized break-policy trial, with workload tracked, would provide stronger causal evidence.”

**Feedback statement 2:**  
“The survey proves breaks raise productivity; because the pattern is clear, workload does not need to be considered.”

## Scope and limitations

This is a small six-pair pilot task bank. It is not a powered confirmatory study. Reviewer agreement is corroboration, not proof of validity. Reviews should be retained with provenance and should not be presented as evidence that NEXUS improves model performance.

The evaluator-only answer keys, scoring criteria, and condition assignments remain in the private research archive. No model/participant testing is authorized. The $0 execution requirement remains absolute.
